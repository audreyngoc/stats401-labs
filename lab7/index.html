STATS 401: Data Acquisition and Visualization

Lab 7: Temporal Network Visualization

This visualization shows how commercial transactions among companies evolve over 60 days. Companies are represented as nodes, while commercial relationships are represented as links. The network changes over time as relationships appear, disappear, and change in transaction activity.

The datasets are fabricated for visualization practice and do not represent real companies or transactions.

Data Structure

Company data: Company ID, company name, sector, and region.

Transaction data: Date, day, source company, target company, transaction amount, transaction type, and transaction count.

Network structure: Companies are represented as nodes and commercial transactions are represented as undirected links between companies.

Animated Commercial Network

The network displays the commercial relationships active on the selected day. Company nodes remain persistent across time to help preserve the mental map, while links enter and exit as relationships change.

Design Description

1. Visual channels. Node color represents company sector. Node shape represents geographic region. Node size represents current transaction volume. Link width represents transaction amount, while link opacity represents transaction count.

2. Temporal encoding. Time is represented through animation, the interactive time slider, and the date selector. As the selected day changes, active commercial relationships are updated. New relationships fade into the network and inactive relationships fade out.

3. Mental map preservation. The same company nodes are maintained throughout the animation instead of recreating the entire network for every day. The force simulation is gently restarted when links change. This helps users follow companies while the network evolves.

Temporal Network Questions

1. How does overall network connectivity change across the 60 days?

Overall connectivity fluctuates across the 60 days, but the network becomes noticeably denser toward the later period. Earlier dates generally contain fewer active relationships and more isolated companies, while later dates show broader participation. For example, on Day 3, there are 3 active links and 6 active companies, compared with 10 active links and 12 active companies on Day 57, showing substantially greater connectivity later in the period.

2. Which companies become more central or commercially active over time?

Fusion Electronics, Ion Systems, Cedar Retail, and Harbor Shipping become particularly prominent. Their large node sizes indicate high transaction volumes, while their multiple connections indicate important positions in the network. All of them become very active commercially while Ion Systems, Fusion Electronics and Harbor Shipping move to the central area of the chart over time.

3. Are there periods when the network consists of relatively separate clusters?

Yes. The network is relatively fragmented during some earlier periods, with several small groups and isolated companies. I also observed a recurring pattern in which a busy transaction day with many high-value relationships is sometimes followed by a less connected day, when the network breaks into smaller clusters. For example, on Day 10, the network has 8 active links connecting 11 companies, whereas on Day 11, only 3 active links connecting 6 companies remain, resulting in several smaller and more separated clusters. This suggests that periods of intensive commercial activity can be followed by short-term fragmentation in the network. However, this pattern becomes less common around Day 40 and afterward. In the later period, relationships more often reconnect shortly after a highly active transaction day, suggesting greater continuity in the network structure.

4. Which commercial relationships appear, disappear, or become stronger over time?

Commercial relationships change substantially throughout the period. Some disappear while new relationships emerge, and link width and opacity show changes in transaction amount and transaction count. For example, the Harbor Shipping–Juniper Stores relationship is visible on Day 9 but is absent in later snapshots. By Day 59, new relationships such as Ion Systems–Apex Textiles and Fusion Electronics–Ion Systems are visible.

5. Does the network become more or less cross-regional over time?

Cross-regional connectivity becomes more prominent toward the later period. Connections between differently shaped nodes indicate transactions between companies from different geographic regions. For example, on Day 9, Granite Wholesale (Europe) connects with Keystone Materials (Asia). By Day 59, multiple cross-regional relationships are visible, including Ion Systems (North America)– Apex Textiles (Asia) and Fusion Electronics (Asia)–Ion Systems (North America).

Dataset Note

The company and transaction datasets are fabricated for visualization practice. They do not represent real companies, transactions, or commercial activity.
