STATS 401: Data Acquisition and Visualization

Lab 6: Hierarchical Data Visualization

This visualization uses the provided GDP dataset to show the hierarchical relationship between continent, area, and country. GDP amount represents the quantitative value used to determine rectangle size, while GDP status indicates whether a country's GDP increased, remained unchanged, or decreased.

Data Structure

Hierarchy: World → Continent → Area → Country

Quantitative attribute: GDP in billions of U.S. dollars

Categorical attribute: GDP status (Increase, Unchanged, Decrease)

Treemap 1: Squarify

The Squarify tiling method attempts to create rectangles with aspect ratios closer to squares, making GDP differences and individual country regions easier to compare and read.

Treemap 2: Binary

The Binary tiling method recursively divides the available space into two groups, creating a clear partition of the hierarchy while producing a different spatial arrangement from Squarify.

Design Description

1. Visual channels. Rectangle area was chosen to represent GDP amount because it allows countries with larger GDPs to occupy more space, making relative differences in economic size easy to compare. Color was chosen to represent GDP status because the three categorical outcomes can be distinguished quickly: green represents an increase, gray represents unchanged GDP, and red represents a decrease. The nested rectangles and labels represent the Continent → Area → Country hierarchy, showing how individual countries belong to larger geographic groups.

2. Segmentation strategies. The Squarify treemap creates more compact, square-like rectangles, which improves readability and makes country areas easier to compare. In contrast, the Binary treemap recursively divides the available space into two groups, creating a clearer sense of hierarchical partitioning but producing more elongated rectangles in some areas. As a result, Squarify is more effective for comparing GDP sizes and reading labels, while Binary emphasizes the structure and organization of the hierarchy.
