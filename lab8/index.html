Mapping the DKU Bulletin

Exploring the relationship between what a university bulletin says and how its information is organized.

This project transforms the Duke Kunshan University Undergraduate Instruction Bulletin into an interactive text corpus. Each point represents a paragraph or short policy passage. Semantic embeddings reveal passages with similar content, while a topic-by-section matrix shows where those themes occur in the document's formal structure.

05 / METHODS & LIMITATIONS

How to interpret this visualization

The methods explain how the map was constructed; the limitations explain what it can and cannot tell us.

Design description

The Duke Kunshan University Undergraduate Instruction Bulletin (2021–2022) was divided into paragraphs and short policy blocks. Chapter, section, subsection, page, and passage metadata preserve each unit's formal location. After text cleaning, all-MiniLM-L6-v2 generated high-dimensional semantic embeddings. UMAP projected the embeddings into two dimensions using 15 neighbors, a minimum distance of 0.15, cosine distance, and a fixed random seed of 401. K-Means clustered the original embeddings into eight groups. TF-IDF terms and representative passages informed the topic labels.

The map uses position to represent the UMAP projection, color to identify semantic topics, and point size to encode passage length. Search and section/topic filters support focused exploration; clicking a passage reveals its source text and nearest semantic neighbors. The Topic × Section matrix summarizes how topics are distributed across formal sections, and selecting a cell connects the matrix to matching points in the map. Together, these views let readers compare semantic similarity with document organization.

The projection is approximate: nearby points can suggest semantic similarity, but the axes are not directly interpretable. Cluster labels are interpretations of model-generated groups, not official bulletin categories. Raw counts can favor longer sections. The visualization therefore supports investigation rather than proving that a topic is important or that two passages are equivalent.

READ WITH CARE

Three interpretation rules

01
Similarity is not identity

Related passages may differ in meaning, conditions, or policy consequences.

02
Clusters are model-derived

Topic names summarize groups; they are not official university classifications.

03
Counts depend on document structure

A section with more passages may dominate raw counts without being more important.
