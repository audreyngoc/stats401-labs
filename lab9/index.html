01 / DATA & CONTEXT

Understanding Global Economic Size

How does the world economy look when economic output, rather than physical territory, determines what we see? This lab explores that question through two coordinated visualizations of the 50 economies with the highest 2025 nominal gross domestic product (GDP) in the supplied dataset. The choropleth encodes GDP using color while preserving geographic boundaries. The cartogram encodes GDP through country area, making differences in economic magnitude more visually prominent.

The dataset, lab9_gdp_2025_top50.csv, contains four variables: iso3, a three-letter geographic identifier; country, the economy's name; gdp_2025_billion_usd, nominal GDP in billions of U.S. dollars; and rank, its position among the included economies. GDP records are joined to geographic features using ISO-3 identifiers so that the same economic values can be compared across both maps.

The visualizations are coordinated through linked highlighting: hovering over a country in either map highlights its counterpart in the other. This interaction helps viewers match countries even when the cartogram's distorted shapes make familiar geographic outlines difficult to recognize.

Dataset and Method

Measure: 2025 nominal GDP at current prices, measured in billions of U.S. dollars. Coverage: the 50 economies in the provided dataset. GDP rank refers to these included economies.

Source: IMF World Economic Outlook, October 2025, nominal-GDP series. WEO observations may include IMF staff estimates where complete official data are unavailable and may be revised in subsequent releases. The values should therefore be understood as estimates for the 2025 reference year rather than permanently fixed measurements.

GDP values are matched to world geographic features using ISO-3 identifiers. Countries outside the supplied top 50 are classified as no data, not as having zero GDP. Their neutral appearance indicates that their GDP values are absent from this particular dataset; it does not imply that these economies have no economic activity.

The two maps use different visual encodings for the same quantitative measure. This makes it possible to compare how color and area influence the perception of economic size while distinguishing the geographic context preserved by a conventional map from the spatial distortion introduced by a cartogram.

02 / COLOR ENCODING

2025 GDP Choropleth

The choropleth preserves countries' geographic boundaries and approximate locations while using color intensity to represent 2025 nominal GDP. Darker blue indicates a higher GDP value, while neutral gray identifies countries without GDP values in the supplied top-50 dataset. This encoding allows viewers to recognize countries, locate economies, and examine the regional distribution of economic output without changing the underlying geography.

GDP values vary substantially in magnitude, so a linear color scale would allocate most of its range to the largest economies. This could make differences among smaller included economies difficult to distinguish. A logarithmic scale spreads out lower values and makes a wider range of economies visually distinguishable. The legend provides quantitative reference points for interpreting the colors. Because the scale is logarithmic, equal visual steps correspond to multiplicative rather than equal absolute differences in GDP.

Metric: Nominal GDP Unit: Billion USD Scale: Logarithmic

03 / AREA ENCODING

2025 GDP Cartogram

Unlike the choropleth, the cartogram changes the apparent area of countries to represent nominal GDP rather than physical land area. Economies with larger GDP values should occupy more visual area, making their relative economic weight easier to compare. Neutral-colored countries have no GDP value in the supplied dataset and should not be interpreted as having zero GDP.

This transformation shifts attention from where countries are located to how much economic output they represent. However, enlarging and shrinking countries requires geographic distortion: familiar shapes, distances, and relative positions become less reliable. The cartogram is therefore most useful for comparing economic magnitude, while the choropleth is better suited to geographic lookup and regional analysis.

04 / DESIGN REFLECTION

Analyze and Compare

How Visual Encoding Shapes Our Perception of Global GDP

In the choropleth, the United States and China are the most visually prominent economies because their high GDP values are represented by the darkest blue shades. Other major economies, including Germany, Japan, and India, can be compared through differences in color intensity. In the cartogram, the United States and China become especially dominant in visual area, while smaller economies occupy less space. This makes differences in economic magnitude more immediate than when comparing colors alone.

The choropleth preserves country boundaries, approximate locations, and regional relationships, making it useful for identifying countries, locating economies, and examining geographic patterns in economic output. Its logarithmic color scale helps distinguish GDP values across a highly skewed distribution, although color differences can still be difficult to compare precisely. The cartogram instead uses area to represent GDP, shifting attention from physical territory to economic weight. However, its distorted shapes, distances, and relative positions make geographic identification and location-based comparisons less reliable.

Changing from color to area changes how global economic inequality is perceived: countries with large economies become visually dominant regardless of their physical size. The choropleth is better suited to geographic and regional questions, whereas the cartogram is more effective for comparing relative economic magnitude. Linked highlighting helps users identify the same economy in both views. Neither representation is universally superior; together, they balance geographic fidelity with a more immediate understanding of economic differences.
